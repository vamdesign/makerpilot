H2 — keep
The Problem Worth Solving
Paragraph 1 — Tory's world
Tory runs her ceramics business alongside a full-time job. She sells on Etsy, does up to six craft shows a year, and stocks pieces through a local cafe. Everything is handmade with a three to four week production cycle. Inventory lives in her head, a notes app, and occasionally a spreadsheet she means to update.
Paragraph 2 — the failure moment
After a show, she boxes up what did not sell and drives home. The online listings do not get updated. A week later, a customer orders something she no longer has. With a three to four week lead time, a missed update is a missed sale — and possibly a missed customer who does not come back.
Paragraph 3 — the insight
The problem is not that Tory lacks discipline. It is that every tool available to her was designed for a desktop, a warehouse, or a business with staff. She needs something she can open at a table between customers, record three sales in under a minute, and put back in her pocket. That gap is where MakerPilot starts.

Insight cards — rewrite all three
Free tier is a strategic constraint
The 10-item cap is not arbitrary. Tory has 100 Etsy listings. She hits the wall on day one. That is intentional: the free tier creates real upgrade pressure without feeling punitive, and it doubles as the demo entry point for anyone discovering MakerPilot through this portfolio.
$9 sits below the pain threshold
Most Etsy sellers already pay $10 to $15 a month for tools like Canva, Later, or Marmalead. The Maker tier at $9 sits below that. It reads as a utility bill, not a software subscription. The moment a price crosses $15, it becomes a decision. That is a line worth staying under.
AI pricing is margin math, not ambition
Claude API and MCP server hosting run $8 to $12 a month at light usage. A $39 Pilot Pro tier barely covers costs. At $59 with 25 users, the AI tier contributes real margin. That math only works if the feature genuinely earns it — which is why AI assistance is scoped to restocking planning, not sprinkled everywhere.
